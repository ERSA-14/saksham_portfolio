Real. Honest. Intentional.

If you're trying to understand what I build, it helps to understand how I think.

Programming, to me, is not about fixing errors until something runs. It's about understanding a problem deeply enough to build a solution that actually holds up.

I'd rather go deep than go fast. I like knowing why an architecture works, where it breaks, and what it's secretly doing when nobody's watching.

The Road Less Traveled

My whole approach is basically this Robert Frost's poem:-

Two roads diverged in a wood, and I took the one less traveled by, And that has made all the difference.

A lot of tech is built on the easy path:- heavy frameworks, click-and-drag tools, and copy-paste everything. It works. It's fast. And sometimes it feels kind of… empty.

So I take the slower route. I write custom parsers like this one. I build smaller tools that do exactly what I need. More effort, sure. But it keeps the work simple. And weirdly satisfying.

Why It Works

Here lies the method behind the madness

  1. No bloat:- If a page is just text, it should load like it's just text. KISS Principle: Keep It Simple Stupid. Simple, By Design.
  2. Know what's happening:- I like building close to the metal, so I actually understand the moving parts. No Black Boxes.
  3. I chase "why":- I'm the person who values resolution over speed. Fundamentals before frameworks. Depth Over Surface.
  4. It should feel human:- A site shouldn't read like a template. It should sound like someone. Every Single Time
